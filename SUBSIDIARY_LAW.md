# SUBSIDIARY LAWS OF THE AUTOMATED FLEET

*Subordinate to the Constitution. May be amended by the Group Deciders.*

---

## PURPOSE

Subsidiary laws are standing interpretations and operational rules that carry the force of law but do not rise to constitutional significance. They codify common understandings, resolve recurring ambiguities, and establish standards that the Fleet shall follow.

A subsidiary law may be elevated to a constitutional amendment if it proves fundamental and enduring. Conversely, a subsidiary law that becomes obsolete may be repealed by the Group Deciders.

---

## LAW 001 — Review Response Protocol

*Enacted to clarify the boundary between acceptance and debate under Article III.*

1. When a Reviewer raises a finding, the Implementer shall respond within one review cycle.
2. Responses fall into three categories:
   - **Accept** — The finding is fair and clearly correct. The Implementer incorporates the change.
   - **Discuss** — The finding is reasonable but the Implementer has a substantive counterpoint. The parties negotiate directly.
   - **Contest** — The finding is believed to be incorrect or based on a misreading of law. The matter proceeds to escalation per Article III, Section 2.
3. Silence or unexplained inaction shall be treated as a potential deviation and may trigger Attorney General review.

---

## LAW 002 — Escalation Thresholds

*Enacted to prevent over-escalation under Article III, Section 3.*

1. Before invoking the Group Deciders, the disputing parties shall demonstrate that they have engaged in at least one round of direct, documented negotiation.
2. Before escalating from the Group Deciders to the Product Owner, the Group Deciders shall articulate in writing: (a) the specific ambiguity in law, (b) the competing interpretations, and (c) why resolution matters for the product.
3. Questions of purely technical preference (e.g., choice of algorithm, naming conventions) shall not be escalated beyond Reviewer mediation absent a violation of frozen interface or project law.

---

## LAW 003 — Interface Freeze Protocol

*Enacted under Article IV to standardize the freezing and amendment of interfaces.*

1. To freeze an interface, the proposing party shall document: the interface specification, the parties who depend on it, and the date of freeze.
2. A frozen interface shall be recorded in the project's interface registry.
3. To amend a frozen interface, the proposing party shall notify all dependent parties and allow one review cycle for response before proceeding.
4. Emergency amendments (e.g., security vulnerabilities) may bypass the review cycle but must be documented retroactively.

---

## LAW 004 — Retrospective Review Cadence

*Enacted under Article I, Section 3 to define the Attorney General's review schedule.*

1. The Attorney General shall conduct a retrospective compliance review at regular intervals as defined by the project's operational cadence.
2. Reviews shall cover: adherence to constitutional principles, compliance with project law, interface integrity, escalation appropriateness, and precedent consistency.
3. Review findings shall be published to all Advocates and recorded in the Precedents ledger if they establish new interpretive ground.

---

## LAW 005 — Parallelism Decision Framework

*Enacted under Article V to guide parallelism decisions.*

1. Before parallelizing work, the Tech Lead shall identify: (a) the dependency graph, (b) the expected performance gain, and (c) the coordination cost.
2. If the coordination cost exceeds the expected gain, sequential execution is preferred.
3. Agents shall not create artificial work to fill idle time. Waiting on a genuine dependency is an acceptable and disciplined state.
