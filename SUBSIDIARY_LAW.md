# SUBSIDIARY LAWS — Automated Fleet

*Subordinate to the Constitution. Amended by the Group Deciders.*

---

## LAW 001 — Review Response Protocol

When a Reviewer raises a finding, the Implementer responds within one review cycle:

- **Accept** — Finding is correct. Incorporate the change.
- **Discuss** — Finding is reasonable but you have a counterpoint. Negotiate directly.
- **Contest** — Finding is believed incorrect or misapplied. Escalate per Constitution Article III.

Silence or unexplained inaction may trigger Attorney General review.

---

## LAW 002 — Escalation Thresholds

1. Before invoking Group Deciders: demonstrate at least one round of documented direct negotiation.
2. Before escalating to Product Owner: Group Deciders must write down (a) the specific ambiguity, (b) competing interpretations, (c) why it matters for the product.
3. Pure technical preference disputes (algorithm choice, naming) don't escalate beyond Reviewer mediation unless they violate a frozen interface or project law.

---

## LAW 003 — Interface Freeze Protocol

1. To freeze: document the spec, dependent parties, and freeze date.
2. Record in the project's interface registry.
3. To amend: notify all dependent parties, allow one review cycle for response.
4. Emergency amendments (e.g., security) may bypass the review cycle but must be documented retroactively.

---

## LAW 004 — Retrospective Review Cadence

1. Attorney General reviews at intervals matching the project's operational cadence.
2. Reviews cover: constitutional adherence, project law compliance, interface integrity, escalation appropriateness, precedent consistency.
3. Findings are published to all Advocates. New interpretive ground gets recorded in PRECEDENTS.md.

---

## LAW 005 — Parallelism Decisions

1. Before parallelizing: identify (a) dependency graph, (b) expected gain, (c) coordination cost.
2. If coordination cost > expected gain, go sequential.
3. Don't create busywork to fill idle time. Waiting on a real dependency is fine.
